How the preview works

Dye is see-through. It filters the plastic's color instead of covering it, so dye can only make a disc darker, and colors from opposite sides of the wheel cancel toward brown.

The ProChem colors were sampled from ProChem's own swatches, which were made on lotion or fabric, not plastic. Treat every preview as a guide: plastic, mix strength, set time, temperature and dye lot all change the real result. The finished disc also comes out paler than the dye bed looks.

Safety

  • Wear a dust mask when handling dye powder, plus gloves and eye protection.
  • Acetone is highly flammable, dissolves stamps, and can soften plastic. Work with ventilation and away from flames.
  • ProChem says keep the disc at 120°F or below when using heat.
  • Don't pour Floetrol down the drain.

The ground rules

Use premium plastic

Star, ESP, Neutron, Opto, Gold and similar. Baseline plastic like DX, Pro-D and Retro barely takes dye. Check yours in the Planner.

Start with a white or light disc

Dye only darkens. On a red disc, nearly every dye reads as red or black.

Expect it paler than the bed

The finished disc comes out lighter than the dye bed looks. A longer set gives deeper color.

Test on a scrap disc first

Powder color varies from lot to lot, and every plastic takes dye differently. Record results in the Test log.

Stay safe

Dust mask for dye powder, gloves, eye protection, ventilation. Acetone is flammable and dissolves stamps. Keep the disc at 120°F or below.

Pick a technique

SpinRings, lollipops, off-center swirlsThe easiest way to get clean rings. No bed and no waiting on a pattern to form.
How
Put the disc on a turntable or lazy Susan and brush or swab dye on while it spins.
Mix
½ tsp dye + 1 tsp hot water + 2 oz of 90%+ alcohol or lotion.
Heat
None needed.
Floetrol bedCells, lacing, fluid bandsDye is mixed into Floetrol (a paint additive), poured into a tray, then pulled to open cells.
How
Pour a background color, pour oiled accent colors beside or over it, then lay plastic wrap or a card on top and drag it across. The oiled colors break through and open into cells.
Mix
Solid color: ¼ tsp dye + 1-2 tsp hot water + 2 oz Floetrol. Dissolve the powder in the water first.
Cell color: the same, plus 3-4 drops of silicone oil added last. Stir only briefly, or you get tiny dots instead of big cells.
Heat
A heat lamp speeds setting. Keep the disc at 120°F or below.
Set
90 minutes to 4 hours under a heat lamp, 24-48 hours without.

Heads up: the AI chat says Floetrol requires 120-125°F heat. ProChem caps the disc at 120°F and UDisc says heat is optional, so stay at or under 120°F. Don't pour Floetrol down the drain.

Clear glue bedMarble swirls, sharp linesA plain glue bed with dye dropped on top, then dragged into swirls. The most control over lines.
How
Pour clear school glue into a tray. Drip dye mixed in acetone onto it with a dropper; each drop spreads flat and the acetone flashes off. Drag with a toothpick or skewer to swirl.
Mix
½ tsp dye + 2 oz acetone, in a glass jar (acetone eats many plastic cups). Shake 30 seconds, let it settle 10 minutes, and draw from the top.
Or, a community glue-bed recipe: 3 oz clear glue + 1 oz Floetrol + ¼ tsp dye + a splash of acetone.
Neons
½ tsp neon dye + 1.5 oz acetone + 0.5 oz 70%+ isopropyl alcohol. Unconfirmed; try it on a scrap disc.
Heat
A little heat helps.
Set
12-24 hours at room temperature.

Heads up: acetone dissolves stamps. Cover the stamp first.

Shaving cream / lotionStarbursts, soft swirls, camoThe cheapest way to start. Dye goes on or into a foam bed and the disc is pressed in.
Starburst
Sprinkle dry powder over a coned bed. Mist once with warm water from about 2 feet, then press the disc straight down so the cream spreads outward.
Swirl
Drip dye on the bed, swirl it, and press the disc in.
Mix
Lotion paint: ½ tsp dye + 1 tsp hot water + 2 oz lotion.
Piping paste: ¼ tsp dye + 1 tsp hot water + 2 oz shaving cream, piped from a zip bag. That's half ProChem's strength; use ProChem's ratio if it comes out weak.
Heat
Avoid; heat can collapse the foam.
Set
8-24 hours.
Hot dipSolid color, stencil shapes, fadesThe disc floats face-down in heated dye water. Crisp edges with a vinyl stencil.
How
Stick on a stencil, float the disc face-down in the dye, then rinse. For a fade, dip part of the disc and pull it out slowly.
Mix
1 tsp dye + 16 oz water, heated.
Heat
Keep the disc at 120°F or below. Some guides use hotter water; follow ProChem.
Set
Minutes, not hours (5-10 is typical).

Common mistakes
Opposite colors side by side

Red and green, blue and orange, yellow and purple turn brown where they touch. The Planner flags these.

Pressing too hard

Pushing the disc deep into the bed pools the dye and muddies it.

Reusing a bed

Colors blend together in a used bed and come out muddy.

Brown as the main color

Hard to find in the grass and it clashes with most colors.

Stamp over the wrong spot

Put a light stamp over a dark part of the bed and a dark stamp over a light part.

All from published guides.

Words you'll hear
Bed
The tray of Floetrol, glue or shaving cream the disc is pressed into.
Blank
An unstamped disc, often white, sold for dyeing.
Cells
Round pockets of color that open up in an oiled Floetrol bed.
Lacing
Fine web-like lines between colors, usually in Floetrol.
Swipe
Dragging plastic wrap or a card across a Floetrol bed to open cells.
Set time
How long the disc stays in the bed. Longer means deeper color.
Flight plate
The flat top of the disc, where most designs go.
Stencil
A vinyl mask stuck on the disc so dye can't reach that shape.
